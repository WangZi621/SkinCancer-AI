        How SkinScan AI works

        A dermatologist never judges a lesion from the picture alone. Age, body site, and skin type change what a spot is likely to be. SkinScan AI reads the image and those details together.

        Training data

        45,400 dermoscopic and close-up images from the public ISIC Archive, every one confirmed by a biopsy rather than a visual impression. They come from clinics in Europe, the Americas, and Australia.

        Benign20,344moles, benign keratoses, dermatofibromas
        Other skin cancer14,210basal and squamous cell carcinoma
        Melanoma10,846

        The model trained on 36,320 images and was checked on 9,080 it never trained on, over 218 epochs in six stages on an RTX 5070 Ti.

        Results on 9,080 validation images

        AUC, all three classes93.2%
        AUC, melanoma0.940
        Melanoma sensitivity90.0%
        Melanoma specificity81.5%
        Overall accuracy78.6%

        Sensitivity and specificity use a melanoma cutoff of 0.278 instead of the usual 0.5. Missing a melanoma costs far more than an extra referral, so the cutoff trades some false alarms for catching 9 in 10 melanomas.

        What it can't do

        • It does not diagnose. Only a biopsy does. Use the result to help decide who to refer first.
        • Darker skin is under-represented. Only 10 of the 3,219 training records that list a skin type are type V or VI.
        • It expects a dermoscopic close-up of a single lesion. Ordinary phone photos from far away are outside what it learned.
        • The validation set also chose the best training epoch and the cutoff, so real-world numbers may be a little lower. It has not been tested on patients prospectively.

        Privacy

        The model file downloads once, about 22.5 MB, and then every scan runs on your device with LiteRT.js. Photos and patient details are never uploaded. Saved scans live in this browser until you delete them. After the first visit the app also works offline.

        The same INT8 model runs on the SkinScan kiosk, a Raspberry Pi device built for clinics.

        The SkinScan kiosk

        Some clinics have no reliable internet and no spare computer. The kiosk is a self-contained version of SkinScan AI that costs about $300 in parts and runs with no network at all.

        Hardware

        ComputerRaspberry Pi 5, 8 GB
        AI acceleratorHailo-8L, 13 TOPS, on an M.2 HAT+
        CameraRaspberry Pi Camera Module 3
        Screen3.5 inch touchscreen
        Case3D printed
        Parts costAbout $300

        Software

        A Python Flask service runs the INT8 model with LiteRT. A pygame interface shows the result on the touchscreen, and the Pi starts both automatically at boot, so staff only have to plug it in.

        Capture, preprocessing, inference, and display all happen on the board. No image is uploaded anywhere.
