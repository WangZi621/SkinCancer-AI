# Storylines

The one thing: **SkinScan AI looks at a skin spot and the patient together and says how soon to refer, on the device, offline.**
The one thing to remember, in a viewer's words: "It told them which spot to send first, and it ran on a $15 computer."

## A. One lesion, start to finish (recommended, built)

Recommended because one real photo travels through every scene, so a stranger can follow cause and effect without narration.

- **Device and form:** the worked example, cut on the circle. The lens circle is the one shape that holds while worlds change: lesion photo, the app's scope, the model's input, the ring gauge, the logo.
- **What the viewer sees:** eight biopsy-confirmed spots; the lens picks one; the real app reads it and the ring closes red; the camera follows each click in the real UI; the photo and the typed-in details flow into the two branches; the numbers land; the 21 MB model fits in a browser and a $15 board; it keeps working offline and on a phone; the limits; the mark.
- **First two seconds:** eight lesion photos already on screen as a loose contact sheet (frame 0 is not empty); the question types in over them.
- **Beats:** hook (8 spots, the question) → the lens picks Sample 1 → real scan for a patient, camera on each click → Refer urgently → where it looked → saved to her lesion, its scans on one chart → the referral report → details typed by hand → photo + details through the model → trained on 45,400 → results → the same lesion on top of the clinic's home screen → the body map → locked and encrypted → offline → phone → 21 MB, browser and Pi Zero 2 W → limits → mark.
- **Second pass:** the app grew from one page into a product (profiles with a passcode, patients, lesion history, reports), so the same lesion now stays on screen past its result: it is saved, followed and reported, and the clinic view opens on it.
- **Look:** the app's own light clinical world (#f5f7f8, white cards, Geist 650) for the product; the deep teal radial world for "inside the model" and the numbers; the lens circle with a white rim and a thin ring in every scene.
- **Sound:** delivered silent for the narrator's voice; a calm bed and clicks on real cursor clicks would be the addition.

## B. The queue

- **Device and form:** a moment in a clinic, one frame that rebuilds. A queue of eight spots waiting; each is scanned and slides into one of three lanes (refer urgently, refer for review, routine) until the queue is sorted.
- **First two seconds:** eight photos in a line, the first one already being scanned.
- Not built: it needs all eight samples recorded through the app, and it shows less of how the model works.

## C. The smallest computer

- **Device and form:** an endless zoom. Start on a $15 Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, dive into its chip, find the 21 MB model, dive into the model and find the two branches, dive into the scope and find a patient's spot.
- **First two seconds:** a bare green board filling the frame, a ring sweeping around its chip.
- Not built: it puts the hardware ahead of the patient, and the zooms would be drawn rather than real footage.
